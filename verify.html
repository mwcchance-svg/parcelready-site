The verification standard

Data you cannot inspect is data you should not buy.

That sentence runs the whole operation. Here is exactly what happens to a row before it ships, for both families in the catalog.

TRADE PACKS · 01

Checked against the business's own website

Every company in a trade pack was verified one by one on its own site. The published email is transcribed exactly as the business prints it, and the page it came from is recorded in the file next to the address.

TRADE PACKS · 02

Locals only, screened by hand

National chains, franchise locations, and lead generation template sites are excluded. A company's local base has to survive its own website. If a site could not be read during the build, the company is left out rather than listed on directory data.

COUNTY PACKS · 03

Built from the official source

County packs come from the appraisal district's ownership file, the county tax office's weekly roll, and the city's own published permit and case data. Not scraped from a listing site, not resold from another vendor.

COUNTY PACKS · 04

The owner occupied check

Absentee owner lists pass a mailing address check: if the owner's mailing address matches the property address, the record is not an absentee owner and it does not ship. The Collin County verification pass found zero slips.

EVERY PACK · 05

Blank means unverified, never guessed

If a field could not be verified, it ships blank. That is why the Houston HVAC pack carries 43 verified emails out of 137 companies: most small operators do not publish one, and the pack tells you the truth instead of padding the file with invented addresses.

EVERY PACK · 06

Match rates and limits, published

Where records are matched across sources, the match rate is printed on the label: Dallas code cases match an owner on 86 percent of cases, and the unmatched cases ship flagged, never filled in. Every product carries an honest limits section that says what the data cannot tell you.

COUNTY PACKS · 07

The owner behind the entity

Where the county records name an owner behind an LLC or a trust, the pack names that owner exactly as the records show. Resolving who actually owns a property is work the platforms leave to you; where the public record allows it honestly, it is done here.

EVERY PACK · 08

A county that fails is skipped, and named

If a county's records cannot meet this standard, the county is skipped and the skip is stated, rather than the list being sold anyway. Where a pack carries contact data, how each field was checked is printed on the pack page, so the risk call is never quietly handed to you.

EVERY PACK · 09

The counter counts every pack

The records counter on the home page is the sum of the counts printed on the pack pages, counted from the live pack files each time the catalog builds. It includes the Free Sample Pack, whose 100 records are drawn from the paid packs, so those 100 are counted in the sampler as well as in their home packs. Add the pack pages up with a calculator and you get the counter's number.

FRESHNESS

Dated snapshots, on a refresh cycle

Businesses change numbers, sites, and owners. Every pack is a dated snapshot and says so on the label: each pack page and cover prints its source and its pull date, so a snapshot's age is never hidden. Refresh follows each source's own publication cycle: weekly for the tax rolls and the Dallas code cases feed, monthly for the appraisal rolls that publish monthly, quarterly for New York City's PLUTO releases, and on the annual certified roll for Travis County, whose official roll publishes once a year. And the promise attaches to the label, not to freshness: if a pack does not match what its page promised, tell us within 30 days and we will fix it or refund that pack.
